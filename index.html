How I work

技

Six tools and one order of operations. The order matters more than the tools — I have redrawn a screen because the flow was wrong far more often than because the pixels were.

  1. 一

    Map the flow

    Before any screen exists, work out who touches the product, in what order, and where they are standing when they do it. Most bad screens are good screens in the wrong place.

    Service map
  2. 二

    Wireframe

    Grey boxes, real words. Deciding hierarchy while it is still cheap to be wrong, and writing the actual labels rather than lorem, because label length is a layout constraint.

    Low-fidelity screens
  3. 三

    Prototype

    Wire it up so it can be used rather than described. A flow that reads fine as a flat file often turns out to have one screen too many.

    Clickable file
  4. 四

    Interaction and states

    The states nobody asks for and everybody meets: loading, empty, error, disabled, too-long-name. Design them or inherit them.

    Motion and edge cases
  5. 五

    Responsive

    Take it down to a phone and back up again. Anything that only survives at one width was never finished.

About

人

I’m a computer science undergraduate who found my way into design. I like working where technology and creativity meet , understanding what can actually be built while thinking carefully about how it should feel to use.

I came to interface design through drawing. I paint and sketch, and that practice taught me to pay attention to small details, composition, and the patience it takes to make an idea work. I bring the same mindset to digital products: explore, refine, simplify, and keep going until the pieces feel right.

Most of my work so far has been about making everyday experiences easier to navigate from managing a busy restaurant to booking a movie ticket. I’m especially interested in the difficult screens: the ones with real problems to solve, where good design has to be both useful and thoughtful.

I’m currently in my third year at St. Xavier’s College, Kathmandu, looking for opportunities to work on real products, learn from people who challenge my thinking, and grow as a designer.
